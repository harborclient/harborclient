# HarborClient Visual Brand Guide

## Navigation-grade tools for HTTP.

## Brand Personality

Imagine HarborClient as software designed by the companies that build:

- Furuno radar systems
- Garmin Marine electronics
- Raymarine navigation equipment
- Kongsberg bridge systems
- Honeywell avionics
- Leica surveying instruments
- Fluke test equipment
- Tektronix oscilloscopes
- Premium industrial control systems

These companies don't sell software.

They build professional instruments.

HarborClient should feel like one of those instruments.

Not consumer software.

Not startup software.

Not enterprise bureaucracy.

A serious tool for skilled developers.

# Lighting

HarborClient exists across many environments.

Examples include:

- A bright ship bridge overlooking calm water
- A stormy bridge at night
- A research vessel's navigation room
- A clean engineering lab
- A modern developer workstation
- An industrial control room
- A marine electronics showroom
- An operations center
- An aircraft cockpit

Lighting should support the story.

Sometimes that's warm morning light.

Sometimes cool overcast skies.

Sometimes glowing instrumentation at night.

Avoid making every scene feel gloomy or dramatic.

# Design Philosophy

HarborClient is not selling adventure.

It is selling confidence.

Every image should communicate one or more of these ideas:

- I can see what's happening.
- I can trust my tools.
- I understand the system.
- I have everything I need to debug this.
- This tool was designed by people who care about craftsmanship.

The maritime influence exists because navigation and debugging solve the same problem: making good decisions from incomplete information.

---

# Brand Test

Every visual should pass this question:

> **Does this look like a professional instrument used by skilled people?**

If yes, it's on brand.

If it feels like a pirate poster, a military recruitment ad, a submarine movie, or generic SaaS marketing, it's not.
